# Competitive Intelligence

Produce CI that changes decisions, not feature-checklist theater. The goal is always one of: where to differentiate, where parity is mandatory, how to win deals, or what a competitor's move means for the roadmap.

## Before starting

1. **Purpose** — battlecard for sales? roadmap input? exec/board briefing? response to a competitor move? Each has a different template (below).
2. **Competitors in scope** — direct, plus ask whether to include adjacent threats (the platform vendor who could bundle the category, the open-source option, the "do nothing" status quo). The most dangerous competitor is often not on the user's list.
3. **The user's product** — get enough context on their positioning, strengths, and target segment to make the analysis comparative, not just descriptive.

## Research workflow

Always search the live web — competitor websites change weekly. Prioritize, in order of signal value:

1. **Pricing pages and changelogs** — the two most honest pages a competitor publishes. Pricing reveals target segment and packaging strategy; changelogs reveal actual velocity and investment areas.
2. **Job postings** — what they're hiring for is what they'll ship in 6–12 months.
3. **Review sites** (G2, Capterra, app stores, Reddit) — mine the *negative* reviews of competitors: those are your differentiation opportunities. Mine negative reviews of the user's own product too if findable.
4. **Earnings calls / funding announcements** — stated strategy and runway.
5. **Docs and API references** — depth of capability that marketing pages overstate.

Distinguish three confidence levels in everything reported: **observed** (seen directly), **reported** (claimed by a third party), **inferred** (your deduction — label the reasoning).

## Output formats

### Teardown / comparison (roadmap input)

```
# Competitive Analysis: [Us] vs [Competitors] — [Date]
## Bottom line
[3-5 sentences: where we win, where we lose, the one move that matters most]
## Positioning map
[Each player's claimed position vs. actual delivery, in one table]
## Capability comparison
[Matrix on dimensions THAT BUYERS DECIDE ON — derive dimensions from review-site complaints and sales lost-deal reasons, not from feature lists. Rate: ahead / parity / behind / N-A, with evidence]
## Pricing & packaging
[Models, entry points, what's gated where — and what that says about their strategy]
## Momentum signals
[Velocity from changelogs, hiring, funding; trajectory not just snapshot]
## Strategic read
[Their likely next 2-3 moves and why; what would hurt us most]
## Recommendations
[Differentiate here / reach parity there / ignore that — each with rationale tied to evidence]
## Sources & confidence
```

### Battlecard (sales enablement) — one page, ruthlessly scannable

```
# Battlecard: vs [Competitor] — [Date]
**Their pitch in one line** | **Our counter in one line**
## Why we win (top 3, each with a proof point a rep can say aloud)
## Why we lose (top 3, honest — reps lose credibility with happy talk)
## Landmines to plant
[Questions a rep should ask that expose the competitor's weakness]
## Objection handling
[Their claim → our response, for the 5 most common claims]
## Pricing comparison [if public]
## Do NOT say
[Claims that are outdated or unverifiable]
```

### Competitor move assessment (they launched/raised/acquired)

Lead with severity: **threat level (high/medium/low) and why**, what it changes for our strategy, recommended response (often "nothing yet" — say so when true), and what signal to watch next.

## Quality bar

- Comparison dimensions come from buyer decision criteria, not vendor feature lists
- Every "we're ahead/behind" judgment cites evidence
- Includes at least one uncomfortable finding about the user's own product
- Strategic read predicts competitor behavior, not just describes it
- Battlecards fit on one page; if it can't be scanned in 60 seconds, it won't be used
