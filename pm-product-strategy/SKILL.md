# Product Strategy & Positioning

Strategy is a coherent set of choices that concentrates limited resources where they can win — not a list of goals, themes, or everything the team plans to do. The test for every strategy this skill produces: **does it say no to something a reasonable person would say yes to?** If nothing real is forsaken, it's a wish list.

## Before starting

1. **Inputs** — current strategy (if any), market/competitive context, PMF status, company strategy this must ladder into, and the honest constraint set (team size, runway, technical position)
2. **The job** — write new strategy, sharpen existing, position a product, or critique a draft?
3. **Audience and altitude** — company product strategy, product-line strategy, or single-product? Board, exec team, or the whole org?

## Strategy document structure

Built on the strategy kernel (Rumelt) + where-to-play/how-to-win (Lafley/Martin):

```
# Product Strategy: [Product] — [Horizon, e.g., FY27]
## Diagnosis
[The honest read: what's really going on in the market and our position — the 2-3 forces that matter, the critical challenge to overcome. A strategy without a diagnosis is a goal with extra steps.]
## Winning aspiration
[What winning looks like in this horizon — concrete enough to falsify, not "be the leading…"]
## Where we play
[Chosen segments, use cases, geographies — AND the adjacent spaces explicitly declined, named]
## How we win
[The differentiated advantage: why the chosen customer picks us over each real alternative. Must be an advantage competitors can't cheaply copy — name why it's defensible]
## Strategic pillars (3-4 max)
[Each: the choice, why now, what it requires, what proves it's working (leading indicator)]
## What we are not doing
[Specific, painful, named — the section that makes it a strategy]
## Capabilities & systems required
[What must be true organizationally for this to work]
## Risks & assumptions
[What would falsify the diagnosis; pre-committed review triggers]
```

Write it as prose arguments, not bullet fragments — strategy lives in the reasoning. Length target: 2–4 pages. If it can't be summarized aloud in 90 seconds, it's not sharp enough yet; include the 90-second version at the top.

## Vision narrative

When the user wants vision (3–10 year): write it as a short narrative of the customer's changed world, not the product's feature future. Structure: the world today and what's broken → the shift underway → the customer's life when we've succeeded → why us. Keep product mechanics out; keep one memorable through-line in. A vision should make hard prioritization calls easier — test the draft against a real recent prioritization dispute and show how it resolves it.

## Positioning

Use April Dunford's method, in order — the order is the method:

1. **True competitive alternatives** — what customers would actually do instead (often a spreadsheet or an intern, not a rival vendor)
2. **Unique attributes** — what we have that the alternatives don't
3. **Value those attributes enable** — translated to customer outcomes, with proof
4. **Who cares most** — the segment for whom that value is urgent
5. **Market category** — the frame of reference that makes the value obvious (deliberate choice: existing category, subsegment, or new category — new category is the expensive option, flag it)

Deliver as a positioning canvas plus the boilerplate one-liner: For [segment] who [need], [product] is the [category] that [unique value], unlike [alternative], because [proof].

## Strategy critique mode

When reviewing an existing strategy doc, grade it against: Is there a diagnosis or just ambition? Are there real choices (find the "not doing" — if absent, the doc fails)? Is the advantage defensible or just a current feature lead? Do the pillars concentrate or scatter resources? Could the opposite of each "choice" be a strategy someone would argue? (If not, it's a platitude — "be customer-obsessed" fails this; "serve mid-market even at enterprise deals' expense" passes.) Deliver: top strengths, the fatal flaw if one exists, and rewrite suggestions per section.

## Quality bar

- A diagnosis exists and a stranger could repeat it
- "What we're not doing" names things with real constituencies
- How-we-win states why the advantage is durable, not just present
- Every pillar has a leading indicator
- Platitude scan run on final draft: any sentence whose opposite no one would argue gets rewritten or cut
